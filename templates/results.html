RESEARCH DOCUMENT 08

FINAL FINDINGS

“The conclusion the data was never supposed to reach.”

After reviewing the respondent's answers, the observed conversational patterns, and the resulting correlations, the researcher has reached an unexpected conclusion.

The original hypothesis can no longer adequately explain the available evidence.

ORIGINAL HYPOTHESIS

Interpersonal compatibility can be reasonably predicted by established preferences, expectations, and observable characteristics.

STATUS: REJECTED

REVISED HYPOTHESIS

Sometimes, meeting a particular person changes the way we understand what we actually value in another person.

STATUS: SUPPORTED BY OBSERVATION

This is creative writing, not an actual scientific conclusion.

THE UNEXPECTED VARIABLE

A variable outside the original model.

The researcher initially assumed that compatibility would depend on whether he matched a predetermined idea of the kind of person the respondent might choose.

Further observation complicated this assumption.

The researcher may not fit every expected category.

The researcher nevertheless found himself enjoying the respondent's company, conversations, humor, perspective, and presence more than any predetermined category could adequately explain.

RESEARCHER'S PERSONAL OBSERVATION

A question the research design did not anticipate.

At some point, the researcher stopped asking whether he fit the respondent's usual type.

He started asking a different question.

Why does this person matter to me this much?

The answer was not found in appearance, categories, preferences, or statistics.

It was found in the conversations.

In the intellectual banter.

In the chaos.

In the moments that somehow became memorable.

In the person behind all of it.

METHODOLOGICAL LIMITATION

The researcher has identified a significant methodological limitation.

RESEARCHER OBJECTIVITY 0.00% STATUS: COMPROMISED

The researcher can no longer reasonably claim that this investigation is purely academic.

LIMITATION OF STATISTICAL ANALYSIS

Where the data stops.

Statistics can identify patterns.

Statistics can describe correlations.

Statistics can organize observations.

But statistics cannot adequately explain why one particular person becomes important.

The available data is therefore insufficient.

Additional evidence is required.

FINAL RESEARCH NOTE

A different kind of documentation.

The researcher has therefore decided to abandon conventional academic documentation for the next stage of the study.

Some findings are better communicated directly.

The researcher has submitted a primary source.
